Perhentian Islands: A Guide to the Clear Waters and White Sands of Malaysia's Most Beautiful Diving Destination

Overview of the Perhentian Islands: A Diving Paradise in Glass-Clear Waters

The Perhentian Islands lie off the east coast of Malaysia in the state of Terengganu, approximately 21 nautical miles (39 kilometres) from the port of Kuala Besut. The archipelago comprises two main islands, Besar Perhentian and Kecil Perhentian, along with numerous smaller islands. According to Tourism Malaysia data from 2023, the islands attract around 45,000–60,000 international visitors each year, more than 70% of whom are diving or snorkelling enthusiasts.

The archipelago is renowned for its “glass-clear waters”, a term that reflects the exceptional transparency of the sea, which remains at 25–30 metres throughout the year. Visibility ranks second only to Sipadan in South-East Asia. Average water temperatures range from 26°C to 29°C all year round, while the best diving season runs from March to October. From November to February, some beaches close due to the north-east monsoon. According to PADI statistics, the Perhentian Islands boast more than 25 certified dive sites, with depths ranging from 5 to 40 metres, making them suitable for divers at every level, from beginners to Advanced Open Water.

Categories and Features of the Dive Sites

  • Tukang Barong: Widely regarded as the most iconic dive site in the Perhentian Islands, reaching a maximum depth of 40 metres. Grey reef sharks, green turtles and large schools of giant trevally are commonly encountered, while varying currents provide an exciting experience for intermediate and advanced divers.
  • Tokong Laut: Approximately 15 minutes by boat from the northern tip of Kecil Perhentian, this renowned “turtle haven” recorded an average of three to five turtles visible per dive, according to a 2022 marine ecology survey.
  • Crystal Bay: Suitable for beginners, with depths of 5–18 metres, coral gardens across the seabed and abundant marine life, including clownfish and blue rays.

For diving enthusiasts visiting the Perhentian Islands for the first time, it is advisable to book a Discover Scuba Diving (DSD) experience or an Open Water (OW) course through a dive centre on the island that offers instruction in Chinese. This helps to ensure safety and a thorough understanding of the local diving conditions and rules.

Main Diving and Accommodation Options

More than 80% of the accommodation on the Perhentian Islands consists of backpacker hostels and diving resorts. Sea Princess Dive Lodge is particularly well known for offering Chinese-language diving courses throughout the year; full details can be found on its official website. Bubble Dive Lodge specialises in affordable dive-package holidays and is ideal for travellers on a limited budget. Tropical Inn Perhentian, meanwhile, boasts the advantage of having the beach right on its doorstep.

For those seeking a higher standard of accommodation, Alunan Resort on Besar Perhentian was named the Best Boutique Resort of 2023 and features a professional PADI five-star dive centre. All resorts peak in season from May to September, when prices are generally 40–60% higher than during the off-season, so advance booking by at least one month is recommended.

💡 Practical tip: Travelling from Kuala Besut to the Perhentian Islands requires a speedboat journey of approximately 45 minutes. Return boat fares range from RM70 to RM90. Choosing a departure between 8 a.m. and 9 a.m. may help to reduce the risk of seasickness. Remember to bring your diving certification card and to wear your swimming costume or swimwear on arrival so that you can enter the water immediately.

Scuba Diving and Snorkelling: A Crystal-Clear Underwater World

Water visibility around the Perhentian Islands typically reaches 15 to 30 metres, while water temperatures remain between 26°C and 29°C throughout the year. The best diving season runs from March to October each year. According to Dive + Travel Report 2022 data, the area is ranked among the best diving destinations in mainland Malaysia, with a turtle encounter success rate of nearly 80 per cent.

Recommended Snorkelling Hotspots

For visitors who are not confident in the water but would like to explore the underwater world, Shark Bay on the east side of Kecil Perhentian is the most popular choice. The water here is only chest- to waist-deep, and sea turtles are often seen foraging in the shallows. Turtle Bay on Besar Perhentian is also suitable for beginners, with colourful schools of coral fish visible less than 50 metres from the shore. For the best experience, head out before 9 a.m., when there is ample light and fewer people in the water.

Scuba Diving Certification Courses

Visitors wishing to obtain an Open Water Diving Certification (PADI Open Water Diver) in the Perhentians can complete a full three-day course for approximately RM900 to RM1,200 (around HK$1,700 to HK$2,300). The fee includes course materials, equipment hire and four open-water dives. Seahouse Dive Centre is renowned for offering instruction in Chinese and provides one-to-one guidance, making it suitable for Cantonese speakers. Tiny Planet Divers specialises in advanced dive sites such as Little Rocks; further details are available on the relevant page.

For Advanced Diving Enthusiasts

Certified divers can explore deeper dive sites in the south of Perhentian, including Teras Rock (also known as Sugar Loaf), which is open exclusively to Advanced Open Water (AOW) divers. At depths of 30 to 40 metres, there are opportunities to see pelagic white eels and large rabbitfish. Another popular site, K Raya Wreck, lies between the two islands. It is the wreck of a cargo ship sunk in the 1980s and has since become an artificial reef. Further details can be found in the itineraries offered by the various dive centres.

Underwater Ecology Tips

Never touch or stand on coral, as doing so may breach Malaysia’s National Parks Act 1988 and result in a fine of up to RM5,000 (approximately HK$9,500). Divers should wear a rash vest rather than apply chemical sunscreen in order to protect water quality and reduce marine pollution.

Guide to Choosing an Island

The Perhentian Islands consist of two main islands — Large Perhentian (Perhentian Besar) and Small Perhentian (Perhentian Kecil). Although they are only a fifteen-minute boat ride apart, they offer distinctly different holiday experiences. According to Tourism Malaysia’s 2023 data, Large Perhentian has around twenty-five resorts, with a greater supply of beds, while Small Perhentian is mainly home to budget guesthouses priced approximately thirty per cent lower.

Large Perhentian is better suited to families and travellers who prioritise comfort. The calm waters of Long Beach have fine, white sand, with shallow shoals extending almost one hundred metres before reaching deep water, making it an ideal place for beginners to learn to swim or try snorkelling without equipment. Established dive operators on the island, such as Rainbow Seaview Resort, offer one-to-one instruction for diving, providing a higher level of safety. The water at Coral Garden in the north is approximately three to five metres deep, with coral coverage exceeding sixty per cent. Sea turtles and clownfish are commonly seen here.

Small Perhentian is popular with backpackers and travellers on a limited budget. Several locally run guesthouses are clustered around Jetty Beach, including Budget Divers Lodge, which charges more affordable rates. The currents at Wishing Fish are stronger, making it suitable for more experienced snorkellers to take on a challenge. In addition, at dusk, visitors to the northwestern end of the island may see manta rays, while the lucky might spot nurse sharks resting in the shallows.

If diving is the main purpose of your trip, both islands have professional dive operators. Seahorse Dive Center on Large Perhentian and Dream Divers on Small Perhentian are among the larger operators, and both offer PADI-certified courses. Travellers are advised to plan their accommodation locations on the island in advance using Google Maps. During the peak season (June to August), boat tickets should be booked at least two months ahead to avoid being turned away at the port.

  • To save money and meet backpackers from around the world → Choose the Jetty Beach area of Small Perhentian

Whichever island you choose, it is advisable to check the weather forecast before departure. The Perhentian Islands’ main attraction is their water activities. According to 2023 statistics from the Marine Department of Malaysia, more than 250 species of coral fish have been recorded in these waters, placing the islands third for species richness among Malaysia’s islands and making snorkelling and scuba diving essential activities.

Popular snorkelling spots include Shark Bay on the northern side of Perhentian Besar and Coral Garden on Perhentian Kecil. Shark Bay is renowned for frequent sightings of nurse sharks and has a depth of just two to four metres, making it ideal for beginners. Coral Garden is famous for the frequent appearance of sea turtles. A single snorkelling trip costs approximately RM80 to RM120, with half-day excursions including equipment hire. Many travellers report that visibility is best when departing before 9am, with turtle encounter rates reaching over 70 per cent.

Scuba diving enthusiasts can take certification courses or Fun Dives with a locally licensed dive centre. Abu Divers on Perhentian Besar is one of the island’s earliest dive schools and offers PADI certification courses. Its four-day Open Water Diver course starts at approximately RM1,800. Dive Gorr on Perhentian Kecil is renowned for small-group tuition, with each instructor teaching no more than four students, making it ideal for travellers seeking personalised guidance. Notable dive sites include “Turtle Avenue”, where an average of six to eight green turtles can be seen on each dive, and “Sugar Wreck”, which features the wreck of a supply vessel that sank in 1999 and attracts numerous underwater photography enthusiasts.

Those not interested in diving can join a one-day Island Hopping tour, which typically visits Pulau Chebeh and Pulau Susu. Pulau Chebeh is approximately 30 minutes from Perhentian by boat and is renowned for its spectacular karst caves and caverns, while Pulau Susu has been voted the best self-guided snorkelling destination, with its underwater coral reefs remaining well protected. Full-day island-hopping tours cost between RM150 and RM200, with some packages including a packed lunch.

On land, beach activities are equally plentiful. Pasir Panjang on Perhentian Besar is a family-friendly, shallow sandy beach where visitors can walk to the offshore coral reef at low tide. Long Beach on Perhentian Kecil hosts a nightly Fire Show, which accommodation guests can watch free of charge, and also stages beach parties on Fridays. Another popular activity is the night forest tour, which costs approximately RM60 and offers opportunities to observe nocturnal wildlife such as hornbills and flying foxes.

Practical advice: Bring a waterproof pouch for electronic devices, as even short boat journeys often involve spray from speedboats. Book scuba diving courses at least three days in advance, as places may be fully booked during the peak season from March to May.

Practical Information: The Best Time to Visit Perhentian Island, Transport and Accommodation Guide

According to 2023 data from the Malaysian Meteorological Department, the best time to visit the Perhentian Islands is from March to October. During this period, rainfall accounts for only around 15% of the annual total, while underwater visibility can reach more than 30 metres thanks to the clear seawater, making it the ideal time for diving. From November to February, the northeast monsoon brings stronger winds and rougher seas. Around 60% of resorts temporarily close during this period, so it is advisable to avoid visiting at this time of year.

The main route to the Perhentian Islands is to fly from Kuala Lumpur with a connection to Kota Bharu Airport, before travelling on by speedboat. The boat journey from Kota Bharu Airport to the islands takes approximately one hour by road, and the airport is served by several daily flights from international destinations, including Malaysia Airlines and AirAsia. The boat journey from the airport to Kuala Besut Jetty takes around 30 to 45 minutes, with the one-way fare costing between 60 and 100 Malaysian ringgit. During the peak season, it is strongly recommended that you book your boat tickets well in advance, as there are only three scheduled departures each day, at 8 am, 11 am and 2 pm. Travelling without a booking can often result in being stranded when the boats are full.

There is a wide range of accommodation options on the islands, divided into three categories. Budget options such as Perhentian Backpackers hostel offer beds for rent from RM50 to RM80 per night; detailed information on room types is available on the official website. Mid-range options, such as Mimpi Perhentian Resort, offer beachfront rooms for approximately RM200 to RM400 per night, including breakfast, and are suitable for families. For a more luxurious experience, The Barat Perhentian offers all-inclusive accommodation from RM500 per night and has a professional dive centre on site.

It is worth noting that there are no bank ATMs on Perhentian Island, so you are strongly advised to bring enough cash with you. Some resorts accept credit cards, but there is usually an additional surcharge. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Below are answers to the most common questions about travelling to the Perhentian Islands, helping you quickly familiarise yourself with essential information and ensure a smooth journey.

When is the best time to visit?

According to 2023 data from the Malaysian Meteorological Department, the best time to visit the Perhentian Islands is from March to October. During this period, rainfall accounts for only around 15 per cent of the annual total, underwater visibility can reach over 30 metres, and the exceptionally clear waters make it the ideal season for diving. From November to February, the north-east monsoon brings rougher seas and stronger winds, and around 60 per cent of resorts temporarily close. It is therefore best to avoid visiting during this period.

How do you get to the Perhentian Islands?

Travelling from Kuala Lumpur, you will need to take an inland flight or a long-distance coach to Kota Bahru, before transferring to a speedboat bound for the Perhentian Islands. The boat journey takes approximately 30 minutes to one hour, with a one-way fare of around RM60–80. It is advisable to arrange a pick-up service in advance through your accommodation provider to ensure that the journey is safe in relation to the tides.

What qualifications are required for diving activities?

To participate in recreational diving, you must hold an internationally recognised diving certification, such as PADI or SSI, and it is recommended that you carry a diving logbook as proof of your experience. If you do not yet have a certification, you can enrol in an OW (Open Water) course at one of the island’s many diving centres. These courses generally last three to four days and cost around RM1,200–1,500. The season with the best underwater visibility is also particularly favourable for beginners.

What accommodation options are available on the Perhentian Islands?

The islands offer a wide range of accommodation, from budget beach houses to mid-range and luxury resorts. Budget accommodation costs approximately RM100–200 per night, while mid-range resorts typically charge RM300–600. During the peak season (March to October), it is advisable to book your preferred accommodation two to three months in advance. More accommodation options can be found on this guide’s category page.

How should you plan your budget?

For a four-day, three-night trip, the approximate basic costs are as follows: transport (flights and speedboat) RM400–600; accommodation RM300–1,800; diving costs (two to three recreational dives) RM400–600; and food and drink RM60–120 per day. Overall, travelling outside the peak diving season can save you around 20–30 per cent of your total expenditure.
