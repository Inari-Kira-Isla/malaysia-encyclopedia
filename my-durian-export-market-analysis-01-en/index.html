Malaysian Durian Export Market Analysis: In-Depth Report on Demand in China, Hong Kong and Macau

Overview of Malaysia’s Durian Export Market

Malaysia is the world’s second-largest durian producer, with an output of approximately 455,000 tonnes in 2023, worth more than 1.3 billion ringgit. Over 80% of its production was exported to China, Hong Kong and Macau. According to data from the Malaysian Ministry of Agriculture, the value of durian exports grew by 42% between 2022 and 2023, driven primarily by a surge in demand for premium durian in the Chinese market.

Since China approved the import of whole Malaysian durians in September 2022, Hong Kong and Macau have become increasingly important as re-export hubs. In 2023, the value of Malaysia’s durian exports to China reached 2.18 billion ringgit, while Hong Kong imported 83,000 tonnes. Although smaller in scale, the Macau market recorded growth of 67%, indicating the impressive consumer potential of the Greater Bay Area.

In terms of market structure, Musang King remains the most popular variety among Chinese consumers, accounting for more than 55% of total export volume. Other premium varieties, including D24, Red Prawn and Black Thorn, also recorded significant growth. Chinese consumers’ perception of durian has shifted from an occasional novelty to a regular purchase, with repeat-purchase rates in first-tier cities such as Shanghai and Beijing reaching 38%.

China Market: The Largest Growth Engine

In 2023, Malaysia’s durian exports to China reached 2.1 billion ringgit, an increase of approximately 65% compared with the previous year. China has now become Malaysia’s largest single export market for durian. According to China Customs statistics, China imported approximately 1.4 million tonnes of durian in 2023. Malaysia’s share of the Chinese market rose from 8% in 2022 to 15%, with its growth rate surpassing traditional suppliers such as Thailand and Vietnam.

The explosive growth of the Chinese market has been driven by three main factors. First, the widespread adoption of e-commerce platforms has enabled consumers to purchase Malaysian durian directly. Platforms such as Tmall Global, JD Worldwide and Pinduoduo have established dedicated Malaysian durian sections, and searches for Malaysian durian during the 2023 Singles’ Day shopping festival surged 320% year on year. Second, advances in cold-chain logistics have helped resolve the challenges of keeping durian fresh. In first-tier cities such as Guangzhou, Shanghai and Beijing, durian can now reach consumers within 48 hours of being picked from Malaysian orchards. Third, Chinese consumers have become more familiar with premium varieties. Musang King has become synonymous with high-quality durian among Chinese consumers, who are willing to pay a premium of RMB 80–120 per kilogram.

Hong Kong-based traders play an important role as transshipment intermediaries in the Chinese market. In 2023, approximately 25% of Malaysia’s durian exports were transhipped through Hong Kong to mainland China. Hong Kong’s international logistics network and free-port status facilitate durian distribution. They should prepare their stocks three months in advance and conclude cold-chain assurance agreements with cross-border logistics providers. At the same time, exporters should actively apply for “durian export registration” with China Customs to ensure efficient clearance. Hong Kong Market: Premium Fresh Durian Consumption

Hong Kong is one of the cities with the highest per-capita durian consumption in the world. In 2023, approximately 8,000 tonnes of durian were imported, with Malaysian durian accounting for 25% of the total and the market worth around HK$420 million. Hong Kong consumers have exceptionally high standards for durian quality and are willing to pay a premium for top-quality produce, spending 40% more per transaction than consumers in mainland China.

Durian consumption channels in Hong Kong can be divided into three main categories: premium supermarkets, specialist durian shops and online platforms. Wellcome and PARKnSHOP have introduced frozen Malaysian durian pulp and are renowned for their rigorous quality control; specialist retailers such as Durian Garden and Thai Elephant Durian sell whole fruits with same-day delivery, emphasising direct-from-origin sourcing. In addition, food delivery platforms such as Deliveroo and Foodpanda have entered the durian delivery market, catering to the needs of younger consumers.

Hong Kong consumers display a strong quality-oriented preference. They favour premium varieties such as Musang King and Black Thorn, and have exacting requirements concerning sugar content, fibre and texture. They also place great importance on product traceability and are willing to choose suppliers that hold Malaysian agricultural produce certification. Exporters are therefore advised to adopt the following strategies: first, establish exclusive partnerships with premium Hong Kong supermarkets and introduce frozen preservation technologies to safeguard quality; second, launch limited-edition premium gift boxes through specialist retailers, highlighting their scarcity; and third, leverage Hong Kong’s position as an Asia-Pacific logistics hub to optimise cold-chain delivery efficiency.

Macau Market: Casino Hotels and Luxury Retail

Macau welcomed approximately 36 million visitor arrivals in 2023, more than 70% of whom came from mainland China. Visitors’ spending power ranks among the highest in the world. According to figures published by the Statistics and Census Service of Macao, the combined revenue of Macao’s gaming and hotel industries exceeded MOP 180 billion in 2023, creating substantial business opportunities for premium imported foodstuffs such as durian. Compared with Hong Kong, Macao has a smaller durian market, with estimated annual imports of around 1,200 tonnes, although the average transaction value is 15% higher than in Hong Kong. This indicates that Macao consumers have a greater willingness to pay for top-tier quality.

Durian distribution in Macao has a distinctive pattern in which casino hotels lead the market and retail channels provide complementary offerings. Premium casino resorts such as Galaxy Integrated Resort and Wynn Palace operate dedicated durian counters offering top-grade varieties from Malaysia, including Musang King and Black Thorn, at average prices of MOP 280–350 per kilogram. High-end supermarkets within The Londoner Macao have also introduced frozen durian pulp in individually packaged portions to meet hygiene and food-safety standards.

In the luxury retail segment, boutique supermarkets at Galaxy Fashion Walk and Shoppers at The Venetian Macao target high-spending visitors who prefer to buy boxed durian as gifts. Thai Elephant Durian has a flagship store on the Macao Peninsula, offering ready-to-eat durian for consumption on site and attracting both local residents and visitors. Macao residents demonstrate strong brand loyalty to durian, with a repurchase rate of 45%. However, limited distribution channels have left a clear gap in the market.

The central opportunity in the Macao market lies in integrating casino hotels’ water-supply capabilities with visitor consumption scenarios. Malaysian exporters are advised to prioritise discussions with Galaxy Entertainment Group and Wynn Macao regarding durian supply agreements while simultaneously developing channels through airport duty-free stores. For high-end travellers, limited-edition gift boxes and VIP pre-order services could be introduced to enhance brand premium.

Other Asian Markets: Singapore, Taiwan and Japan

As the central hub for durian consumption in Southeast Asia, Singapore’s durian market was estimated at more than SGD 150 million in 2023, with Malaysian Musang King accounting for approximately 35% of the market share. According to the Singapore Food Agency, Singapore imports around 40,000 tonnes of durian annually, with an average retail price of SGD 12–18 per kilogram, higher than in other Southeast Asian countries. Singaporean consumers have strict quality requirements and tend to select suppliers with food safety certifications, giving Malaysian exporters with ISO and HACCP certification a competitive advantage.

In Taiwan, approximately 1,200 tonnes of fresh durian were imported from Malaysia in 2023, representing a 28% increase on 2022 and indicating rapid growth in market demand. Taiwanese consumers prefer sweeter Musang King and Black Thorn varieties and are willing to pay NT$800–1,200 per kilogram for premium quality. However, Taiwan imposes strict quarantine regulations on imported fruit. Exporters must ensure that their products meet the quarantine requirements of Taiwan’s Agricultural Council and obtain a phytosanitary certificate from the country of export.

The Japanese market is still at an early stage of development. Imports of durian to Japan totalled only around 80 tonnes in 2023, but the average annual growth rate reached 15%, indicating long-term potential. Japanese consumers are highly sensitive to food safety issues and require detailed information on product origin and pesticide residue test reports. Exporters should initially target premium supermarkets and upscale food retailers, build brand recognition and then gradually expand their distribution channels.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When is the best time to export to the Singapore market?

The peak season for durian in Singapore runs from June to August each year, coinciding with Malaysia’s durian harvest season. According to data from the Singapore Food Agency, approximately 40,000 tonnes of durian are imported into the country annually, at an average retail price of SGD 12–18 per kilogram. Exporters should establish distribution channels two to three months in advance. It is recommended to discuss potential partnerships with local importers such as Golden Dragon Fruits and Durian Pte Ltd, which have a strong reputation for reliable supply chains. Further information can be found in the feature on durian importers in Singapore.

What are Taiwan’s phytosanitary requirements for durian imported from Malaysia?

Taiwan’s Bureau of Animal and Plant Health Inspection and Quarantine, under the Council of Agriculture, requires imported durian to be accompanied by a plant quarantine certificate and to undergo border inspections. In 2023, Taiwan imported approximately 8,000 tonnes of durian, with Malaysian durian accounting for around 25% of the total. Taiwanese consumers prefer chilled fresh durian, so exporters are advised to use vacuum rapid-freezing technology to preserve the quality of the flesh. Well-known retailers such as Carrefour and PX Mart import Malaysian durian and may be potential business partners.

How high are the barriers to entry in the Japanese market?

Japan operates a strict food safety and quarantine system for imported durian, and all imports must comply with the regulations of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. In 2023, Japan imported approximately 2,000 tonnes of durian, representing a market worth around JPY 5 billion, of which Malaysian durian accounted for only about 5%. Japanese consumers place considerable importance on packaging and brand storytelling, so exporters should invest in differentiated product design. Chain supermarkets such as DON DON DONKI and AEON are the principal distribution channels, and exporters should prioritise discussions about opportunities to secure shelf space.

Which of the three markets has the greatest growth potential?

In terms of growth, the Japanese market has the highest average annual growth rate of the three, at 15%, driven primarily by rising demand for premium food products. However, Taiwan has lower market-entry barriers and a strong sense of cultural affinity, making it a suitable market for small and medium-sized exporters to test the waters. Exporters are advised to adopt a “Taiwan first, then Japan” strategy, gaining experience before entering Japan’s premium market.

How should exporters choose their target markets?

Three key factors should be considered when selecting a market: certification requirements, profit margins and logistical convenience. Singapore requires ISO and HACCP certification, Taiwan focuses on quarantine procedures, while Japan places greater emphasis on quality and packaging. Exporters should initially obtain ISO 22000 certification for their food safety management system. This can help them meet the basic requirements of all three markets while strengthening their international competitiveness.

常見問題

What is the main cost structure for importing durian from Malaysia to Macau?

Import costs include the purchase price, international logistics, customs duties and cold-chain storage. Taking Musang King as an example, the price at origin in Malaysia is approximately RM30–50 per kilogram. After adding airfreight and import duties for Macau, wholesale costs rise by around 40–60%. Consider partnering with direct suppliers such as Ting Ho Fruit to reduce intermediary costs.

How much profit can be made from wholesale durian in Macau?

The wholesale gross margin for durian in Macau is approximately 25–35%, depending on the variety and season. Musang King offers higher margins but has slower capital turnover, while common varieties such as D24 focus on sales volume. Durian imports in Macau grew by 67% in 2023, indicating considerable market potential. It is advisable to diversify investments across different varieties to reduce risk.

How can the consistent quality of a durian supplier be assessed?

A quality supplier should hold certification from the Malaysian Ministry of Agriculture, have rapid-freezing equipment and be able to maintain a reliable supply. Consider visiting production sites in person or working with established wholesalers such as Durian Prince. Ting Ho Fruit provides quality inspection reports, while the rapid-freezing technology used by Sin Ma Durian Farm helps ensure consistent pulp quality.

Can AI tools help a durian retail business?

AI can be applied to inventory forecasting, customer behaviour analysis and social media marketing. For example, analysing historical sales data can help predict demand for the following week and prevent excess stock from causing wastage. The online system operated by the Malaysia Durian Network uses AI algorithms to help retailers optimise purchasing decisions.

What documents and licences are required to import durian into Macau?

The required documents include a Malaysian plant health certificate, a Chinese customs import permit (for example, when goods are transited through Hong Kong) and a Macau Municipal Affairs Bureau import licence. Since China opened its market to whole durians in 2022, the process has been simplified. It is advisable to appoint a customs broker with experience in trade between mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau.

What are the optimal temperature and storage method for durian in Macau?

Whole durians should be stored in refrigeration at 4–8°C, where they can be kept for three to five days. The pulp must be rapidly frozen to -18°C and can then be stored for several months. The rapid-freezing technology used by Sin Ma Durian Farm preserves the texture of the pulp. Retailers should consider installing commercial refrigeration equipment to maintain quality.

How can the repurchase rate for durian products be increased?

The repurchase rate among consumers in mainland China has reached 38%, and this trend can be applied to the Macau market. Strategies include introducing a membership scheme, providing high-quality after-sales service and holding durian tasting events. Durian Prince specialises in the wholesale of Musang King and offers product training to help retailers improve the professionalism of their service.

Who are the competitors in the Macau durian market?

Competitors in the Macau durian market include local fruit wholesalers, supermarkets and cross-border e-commerce platforms. Chain businesses such as Ting Ho Fruit hold a substantial market share. Smaller retailers can differentiate themselves through distinctive varieties, home delivery and other specialised services. The online platform operated by the Malaysia Durian Network is also a potential competitor.

What are the main risks in the durian import business, and how can they be mitigated?

The main risks are price volatility, wastage and changes in government policy. Durian prices are affected by weather conditions and output, with fluctuations of up to 30%. Consider signing long-term contracts to lock in prices, sourcing multiple varieties to diversify procurement and monitoring developments in trade policy between China and Malaysia. Changes to Chinese customs inspection standards could also affect the supply chain.

How can social media be used to promote a durian business?

Facebook, Instagram and Xiaohongshu can be used to showcase the process of opening durians and share tutorial videos that attract traffic. Combining these posts with AI-generated copy and images can improve publishing efficiency. The social media marketing strategy used by Ting Ho Fruit is worth emulating, particularly in terms of building a brand identity and engaging with customers.
