Malaysia Live-Stream Shopping: The Complete Guide to Food-Category Live-Commerce

The State of Malaysia’s Livestream Shopping Market in 2026

According to industry reports, Malaysia’s livestream e-commerce market surpassed RM3.5 billion in 2025, representing growth of more than 60% compared with 2024. It is expected to maintain an annual growth rate of over 50% in 2026. This explosive growth has been driven primarily by rising mobile internet penetration, which currently exceeds 85%, and by Gen Z consumers shifting their shopping habits from traditional e-commerce to interactive livestreams.

In terms of platform preference, Shopee Live, TikTok Shop and Facebook Live have emerged as the three leading channels, collectively accounting for more than 90% of the livestream shopping market. Notably, TikTok Shop achieved a staggering 180% increase in GMV in 2025, with the food and beverage category contributing approximately 40% of its performance and becoming one of the platform’s fastest-growing categories.

Looking at user behaviour, Malaysian consumers spend an average of 8.5 minutes in livestream rooms, considerably longer than the three minutes they spend on traditional e-commerce pages. In terms of conversion rates, food products achieve livestream conversion rates of 12% to 15%, outperforming the 8% to 10% recorded for general merchandise. MyBurger Lab is renowned for limited-time livestream offers featuring creative burger combinations, further details of which can be found on its official page; Teh tarik has attracted Chinese and Malay consumers through livestreams showcasing the traditional art of preparing milk tea; and Happy Bear is known for livestreaming durian products directly from their source. These success stories demonstrate the immense potential of food-focused livestream shopping in Malaysia.

For brands seeking to enter Malaysia’s food livestreaming market, it is recommended that they establish a presence on Shopee Live or TikTok Shop first and develop differentiated content tailored to Malaysia’s multicultural consumer base, including Chinese, Malay and Indian consumers. Malay consumers tend to prefer livestreams featuring halal food, while Chinese consumers are generally more sensitive to product quality and value. Understanding these consumer characteristics is key to success.

Food Live-Commerce: Best Categories and Product Selection Strategies

According to iPrice Group’s 2025 data, food and beverages are the second-best-selling category in Malaysia’s live-commerce market, accounting for 23% of total sales, just behind fashion and apparel. This is because food’s “essential + high-frequency” consumption characteristics give it exceptionally high conversion rates in live-streaming scenarios.

1. Best-Selling Food Category Matrix

Malaysia’s food live-commerce market features three main categories: ready-to-eat foods (such as instant noodles, canned foods and meal packs) account for 35%, snacks (such as biscuits, nuts and preserved fruits) account for 28%, and health and wellness foods (such as ready-to-eat bird’s nest, honey and cereals) account for 22%. Brands such as Mamee Double, Oreo and Healofy have achieved single-session live-stream GMV exceeding MYR100,000 on TikTok Shop with their ready-to-eat and snack products, reflecting Malaysian consumers’ strong demand for products that are “convenient + delicious”.

2. Four Principles for Product Selection

  • Visual impact comes first: Choose products with vibrant colours and distinctive packaging. For example, when Malaysian local brand “Durian King” showcases the moment its durian products are cut open during a live stream, conversion rates can rise by 40%.
  • Setting the price range: An entry-level price of RM19–RM59 (approximately NT$140–440) is recommended. This range can appeal to first-time purchasers while leaving sufficient room for the presenter’s commission.
  • Inventory depth: Food live-commerce campaigns should maintain stock equivalent to at least three times the estimated sales volume, preventing shortages after a sales surge from leading to reduced account visibility.
  • Seasonal opportunities: Launch festive gift boxes in line with Malaysian celebrations, such as Hari Raya Aidilfitri and Chinese New Year. During key festive periods, food live-commerce GMV can be 2–3 times higher than on ordinary days.

3. Product Selection by Platform

TikTok Shop is best suited to products aimed at younger consumers, including casual snacks and items that align with the influencer’s personal brand. Shopee Live is more suitable for family-sized food products and bundles of everyday essentials. Facebook Live is better suited to higher-value health and wellness foods, such as ready-to-eat bird’s nest and reishi spore powder, because its user base tends to be older and is more trust-driven. CT Cake House is renowned for its French-style dessert live streams; further details can be found on its official Shopee store. Marrybrown’s frozen chicken meal packs appeal particularly to families with children; its official TikTok channel is also worth consulting.

Livestream Studio Setup and Technical Requirements

The hardware configuration of a livestream studio directly affects conversion performance in the food category. According to Meltwater’s 2024 Malaysian e-commerce report, viewers spend 47% longer in studios equipped with professional lighting than in those with standard setups, because the presentation of food colours and textures depends heavily on lighting.

Lighting systems: For food livestreaming, the three-point lighting method is recommended. Use a 5600K LED fill light at a 45-degree angle in front of the presenter for the key light, a ring light to eliminate shadows on the face as the fill light, and a small spotlight to highlight the contours of the food as the backlight. An iPhone 14 Pro or Samsung Galaxy S24 series device paired with a mobile phone stand can meet basic requirements. For higher-quality video, consider a Sony ZV-E10 mirrorless camera with a Rode NT-USB microphone. The total equipment investment is approximately MYR 3,000 to MYR 8,000.

Network configuration is a critical bottleneck for livestreaming in Malaysia. Shopee Live recommends an upload speed of at least 15Mbps. Use fibre broadband (such as a Unifi or TIME package) as the main connection, and prepare a 4G/5G mobile broadband hotspot as a backup. Food livestreaming often requires close-up product shots, so a mobile phone gimbal (such as the DJI OM 6) is recommended for filming packaging and tasting demonstrations at close range.

The background design should focus on the food theme. Mytata is renowned for creating livestream studios with a fresh, pastoral style; refer to its official feature for detailed scene design. Cheetay, a leading local snack livestreaming brand, uses multi-level display shelving to showcase its products. Secret Recipe’s cake livestream studio favours a warm-toned kitchen style to enhance the products’ appeal. During livestreams, keep a power bank and a spare mobile phone ready to ensure that a 2–3-hour broadcast is not interrupted.

Presenter Training and Scripts: Professional Techniques for Food Products

Live-streamed food sales achieve conversion rates 32% higher than those of general product categories. This is because consumers can directly experience the flavour of a product through the presenter’s live demonstration and tasting reactions. However, this also places greater demands on the presenter’s professional expertise.

The first step for food presenters is to build a “sensory descriptor bank” covering four dimensions: sight (colour, gloss and texture), smell (aroma intensity and depth), taste (sweetness, saltiness and mouthfeel) and touch (crunchiness and softness). According to an iPrice Group analysis of Malaysia’s live-streaming e-commerce market in 2024, livestreams that use comprehensive sensory descriptions achieve conversion rates 28% higher than those offering only brief product introductions.

A recommended five-stage script structure is “opening – demonstration – tasting – interaction – sales close”. During the opening stage, the presenter should stimulate the audience’s appetite within the first 30 seconds by using scarcity-based phrases such as “today’s selection” or “limited availability”. The demonstration stage should focus on capturing close-up shots of the food, using tight shots to highlight the texture of the ingredients. The tasting stage is crucial for conversion, requiring presenters to display genuine tasting reactions alongside descriptive language, for example: “This jerky is really chewy and packed with flavour.” During the interaction stage, presenters can maintain audience engagement with questions such as: “Which flavour would you like to see me try?” Finally, the sales close should clearly explain the promotional offer and stock availability.

7-Eleven Malaysia is renowned for its livestreams featuring ready-to-eat convenience-store foods, with details of its comprehensive training system available on its official website. Secret Recipe differentiates itself through livestreams focused on baked goods and desserts, while its related features can be found in the Malaysia bakery livestreaming section. ZUS Coffee is particularly known for the design of its coffee and tea tasting segments. Marrybrown’s fast-food livestreams stand out through value-for-money meal bundles, while QSR Brands excels in standardised scripts across its restaurant chains.

In practical terms, presenters are advised to spend 30 minutes each day learning about the products and recording trial broadcasts to review their fluency and body language. Cross-Border Live-Streaming: Malaysia → Macau/Hong Kong Markets

Malaysian food products are entering the Macau and Hong Kong markets through live-streaming e-commerce and are in a period of rapid growth. According to eMarketer’s 2024 forecast, live-streaming e-commerce GMV in Hong Kong and Macau is growing at an annual rate of 47%. The food and beverage category accounts for 31% of total transaction value, indicating continued strong demand in South China for high-quality food products.

Market Opportunities and Consumer Preferences

Consumers in Macau and Hong Kong have a high level of recognition for Malaysian food, particularly bak kut teh, white coffee, bird’s nest products and processed tropical fruits. According to the Alipay Cross-Border Consumption Report 2024, searches for Malaysian food in Hong Kong and Macau increased by 68% year on year, while the average order value was HK$280, 22% higher than for locally produced food.

Platform Selection and Market Entry Strategies

The main cross-border live-streaming platforms include TikTok Shop Hong Kong, Facebook Live Shopping and WeChat Mini Programme live-streaming. TikTok Shop officially opened to Malaysian merchants seeking to enter the Hong Kong market in 2024, offering cross-border logistics subsidies and a 50% reduction in delivery fees for the first three months. Facebook, meanwhile, benefits from its established user base in Hong Kong and Macau and is well suited to building private-domain traffic.

Operational recommendation: Consider adopting a “simultaneous streaming across two platforms” strategy, with TikTok Shop attracting new customers and Facebook Live supporting private-domain conversion among existing members. Both platforms can share the same product inventory and order system.

Regulatory and Customs Clearance Requirements

Cross-border live-streaming of food products must comply with the following requirements: imported food must obtain a Hong Kong Centre for Food Safety import licence and submit an advance import notification to the Macao Municipal Affairs Bureau. Product labels must meet the traditional Chinese language and ingredient-labelling requirements of Hong Kong and Macau. Products such as bird’s nest and dried seafood must be accompanied by health certificates from their country of origin. It is advisable to work with local customs clearance agents, such as DHL Express Hong Kong and SF International, which provide dedicated customs declaration channels for food products.

Payment and Logistics Solutions

Consumers in Hong Kong and Macau commonly use Octopus, AlipayHK and WeChat Pay HK for payments. Malaysian merchants can open multi-currency payment accounts through PingPong or Airwallex, enabling immediate settlement in Malaysian ringgit. For logistics, consider adopting an “overseas warehouse plus dropshipping” model, establishing a forward warehouse in Kwai Chung, Hong Kong, or Macau to reduce delivery times to two to three days.

Localised Content Strategy

Streamers in Hong Kong and Macau should conduct live streams in Cantonese and adapt their sales scripts to local dietary preferences. For example, when promoting bak kut teh, they could emphasise that it is “suitable for the whole family as a nourishing autumn and winter dish” and “convenient to use straight from the packet as soup”, rather than focusing on the “rich Malaysian cultural experience” commonly used in Malaysia.

Frequently Asked Questions FAQ

1. Which platforms should be chosen for food live-streaming sales in Malaysia?

Live-streaming e-commerce in Hong Kong and Macau is dominated by Shopee Live, LazLive and TikTok Shop. Data from Q3 2024 shows that these three platforms accounted for 78% of total live-streaming GMV in the region. Shopee has the highest penetration among Malaysia’s Chinese-speaking communities, making it suitable for promoting Malaysian speciality foods such as bak kut teh and white coffee; TikTok Shop primarily targets younger consumers and is well suited to wellness products such as bird’s nest products and processed fruit products. Lazada has a higher market share in Macau, making it suitable for mid- to high-priced gift boxes. Beginners should start with Shopee Live and gradually expand to multi-platform operations.

2. How should cross-border logistics be managed?

Delivery times from Malaysia to Hong Kong and Macau usually take 5–7 working days. Mainstream options include air freight express and cold-chain delivery. For food products, shelf life must be considered, and logistics partners with FDA and MESTI certification are recommended. In the initial stage, providers with an extensive presence in Malaysia, such as J&T Express or Flash Express, can be used. Once order volumes have stabilised, partnering with DHL or SF Express may provide access to more competitive cross-border freight rates.

3. Which payment methods do consumers in Hong Kong and Macau accept?

According to Alipay’s cross-border consumer data, 63% of consumers in Hong Kong and Macau prefer electronic payments, with AlipayHK and WeChat Pay HK being the most widely used. Live-streaming platforms should offer one-click payment to reduce friction at the point of purchase. It is advisable to provide a “cash on delivery” option for the 15% of users who have not linked an electronic payment account, as well as support Octopus card top-ups for purchasing premium gift boxes. The payment page should display prices in Hong Kong dollars or Macau patacas to prevent customers from being lost as a result of currency conversion.

4. What certifications are required for food imports?

Hong Kong’s Centre for Food Safety requires imported food to be accompanied by a health certificate from the place of origin, while Macau’s Municipal Affairs Bureau requires importers to submit an import declaration. Malaysian exporters should ensure that their products have obtained MESTI (Malaysian Food Safety Information System) certification and have an ISO 22000 food safety management certificate ready. Bird’s nest products must also appear on the import access list of the General Administration of Customs of China. Certification marks should be clearly displayed in the live stream to enhance consumer confidence.

5. How can product selection be optimised to improve conversion and transaction rates?

Malaysian foods that sell well in Hong Kong and Macau share several distinctive features: convenience (such as instant white coffee and ready-to-eat meal packs of bak kut teh), gift appeal (such as bird’s nest gift boxes and nut gift packs), and differentiation (such as Musang King durian products and speciality snacks such as orchid glutinous rice). According to previous campaigns, products that achieved GMV exceeding HK$100,000 in a single live stream had an average order value of HK$150–300, which helped increase order volumes.
