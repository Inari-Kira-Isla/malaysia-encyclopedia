Complete Guide to Malaysia’s Live Seafood Aquaculture Industry: Sarawak, Sabah, and Pahang

Overview of Malaysia’s Marine Aquaculture Industry

Malaysia’s aquaculture industry has grown steadily in recent years. According to 2023 data from the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), Malaysia’s total aquaculture production reached approximately 410,000 metric tons, valued at around MYR 2.5 billion, with marine aquaculture accounting for approximately 35%. This growth is driven by rising domestic and international demand for premium live seafood. In particular, Chinese consumers’ preference for high-value species such as live lobsters, groupers, and mud crabs provides a steady source of income for farmers.

Geographically, Malaysia’s marine aquaculture industry is concentrated in three key regions: Sibu and Bintulu in Sarawak, Kota Kinabalu and Sandakan in Sabah, and Kuantan and Pekan in Pahang. Sarawak is known for its large-scale offshore cage farming, offering suitable conditions for farming premium live lobsters and silver perch. Sabah’s clear waters and abundant coral reef resources make it well suited to farming live groupers and sea cucumbers. Pahang focuses primarily on coastal pond farming, producing substantial volumes of live mud crabs and white shrimp. Each of these three states has distinct strengths, creating a complementary industry landscape.

Malaysia’s marine aquaculture industry focuses on key products including tiger prawns, Gracilaria seaweed, milkfish, and groupers. According to 2022 statistics from the Malaysian Aquaculture Development Authority (Lembaga Per Akuakultur Malaysia), national farmed tiger prawn production totaled approximately 80,000 metric tons, with around 70% supplied to export markets, primarily Singapore, Hong Kong, and mainland China. Given the stringent requirements for keeping seafood alive and fresh, supply chain management is critical to product quality.

Investors and buyers looking to enter Malaysia’s marine aquaculture industry should prioritize partnerships with local farms that hold export licenses, such as coastal farming communities in Sarawak or offshore cage farming operators in Sabah. These partners are familiar with international logistics procedures and can ensure that live seafood reaches major consumer markets within 24 hours. Live mud crab farms in Pahang also offer relatively low partnership costs, making them suitable suppliers for trial orders. Buyers can verify supply chain reliability before expanding into other high-value species.

Sabah: Malaysia’s Marine Aquaculture Hub

Located in northern Borneo, Sabah has more than 1,400 kilometres of coastline and access to South China Sea waters with temperatures well suited to aquaculture. In 2023, the state accounted for approximately 38% of Malaysia’s farmed marine seafood production, making it the country’s largest marine aquaculture hub. According to the Sabah Aquaculture Association, aquaculture farms in the state cover more than 8,000 hectares, concentrated primarily around Kota Kinabalu, Penampang and Tuaran. Lobster, grouper and cobia are the sector’s three main farmed species.

Sabah’s competitive advantage in marine aquaculture stems from its favourable natural conditions. Average water temperatures remain between 26°C and 30°C, while clean waters with limited industrial pollution support the growth of high-value species. In recent years, the Sabah Aquaculture Department has promoted its “Deep-Sea Cage Farming Programme”, encouraging farmers to transition from traditional nearshore floating cages to offshore cage systems in waters more than 15 metres deep. These systems can increase output per cage by three to five times. In 2022, Sabah exported approximately 2,800 metric tonnes of live lobster, with 70% destined for Hong Kong and mainland China. Average selling prices reached RM120 per kilogram, generating substantial returns for farmers.

However, Sabah’s aquaculture sector also faces challenges. The supply of juvenile stock is heavily dependent on imports, with local hatcheries meeting only around 30% of demand. The typhoon season, from November to March, also poses risks to coastal cages. Farmers may consider joining the Sabah Aquaculture Cooperative to share early warning information and reduce costs through collective feed purchasing. Investment in indoor recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS) can also help minimise weather-related disruption while meeting export markets’ food safety requirements.

Kota Kinabalu and Penampang are home to numerous live seafood restaurants, where visitors can enjoy freshly harvested lobster cooked in butter and steamed grouper. For a comprehensive overview of Sabah’s aquaculture industry, visit the “Sabah Seafood Farms” feature page for contact details of major farmers and information on the species they farm.

Sarawak: Strengths in Freshwater Aquaculture

Sarawak has Malaysia’s most abundant freshwater resources, with more than 50 major rivers and numerous reservoirs across the state. Freshwater fish production reached 52,000 metric tonnes in 2023, accounting for approximately 65% of the state’s total aquaculture output and establishing the sector as a core industry that complements Sabah’s marine aquaculture. According to the Sarawak Fisheries Development Authority, freshwater farms in the state now cover 12,000 hectares, primarily around Kuching, Simunjan, and Sibu.

Key Species and Market Positioning

Sarawak’s freshwater aquaculture sector is built on three main categories: tilapia, Mozambique tilapia, and freshwater prawns. Annual tilapia production exceeds 28,000 metric tonnes, supplying local markets and neighboring countries such as Singapore and Indonesia. Water quality in the state is better than in some parts of Peninsular Malaysia, making it suitable for farming high-value freshwater species such as bass and eels. In recent years, the Bintulu district has introduced indoor recirculating aquaculture systems, improving production efficiency by approximately 40% compared with traditional pond farming.

Investment Advantages and Policy Support

  • The Sarawak government offers preferential land leases for aquaculture, with rent reductions of up to 70% during the first five years
  • Aquatic quarantine centers and export certification services within the state streamline seafood exports
  • Labor costs are approximately 15% lower than in Peninsular Malaysia, making the state suitable for labor-intensive aquaculture operations

Development Recommendations: Investors looking to enter Sarawak’s freshwater aquaculture sector can consider partnering with local aquaculture cooperatives or member companies of organizations such as the Sarawak Aquaculture Association to reduce initial land acquisition costs. Bintulu’s deep-sea aquaculture zone has gradually diversified the species it farms. For details on farm partnership opportunities, consult local fisheries business directories.

According to the Sarawak Fish Farmers Association, freshwater fish export value rose by 22% year on year in the first half of 2024, indicating continued growth in market demand.

While Sabah focuses on marine aquaculture, Sarawak’s freshwater aquaculture sector offers a distinct investment option for investors seeking to diversify their risk. For more information on aquaculture in Sarawak, visit our Sarawak Aquaculture Industry Feature.

Pahang: A Hub for Shrimp Farming

Covering approximately 35,840 square kilometres, Pahang has one of the longest coastlines on Malaysia’s east coast. In 2023, its farmed shrimp production reached 38,000 metric tonnes, accounting for approximately 72% of the state’s total aquaculture output and establishing Pahang as a key shrimp-producing region in Malaysia. According to statistics from the Pahang Fisheries Development Authority, shrimp ponds in the state now cover more than 8,500 hectares, primarily concentrated in the coastal areas of Kuantan, Temerloh and Rompin.

Key Species and Market Positioning

Shrimp farming in Pahang centres on two main species: black tiger shrimp and Pacific white shrimp, also known as whiteleg shrimp. Black tiger shrimp account for approximately 55% of local farmed shrimp production. Prized for their sweet, succulent flesh and thin, easy-to-peel shells, they have long served premium foodservice and export markets. Pacific white shrimp make up the remaining 45% and are known for their rapid growth and consistent yields, making them well suited to processing into frozen shrimp and peeled shrimp products. According to a 2023 report by the Malaysian Fisheries Association, Pahang has approximately 320 licensed shrimp farms, 80% of which are small and medium-sized family-run operations, with an average farming area of approximately 25 hectares.

Geographic Advantages and Industry Ecosystem

Coastal waters in Pahang maintain a stable salinity of 15–25 ppt, providing suitable conditions for shrimp growth. Proximity to Kuantan Port and direct shipping routes also supports efficient logistics. The state has developed a comprehensive supply chain, with specialist operators covering farming, feed supply, processing and exports. Several shrimp processing plants in the Kuantan Industrial Area can handle live shrimp immediately upon arrival from farms, increasing product value. Lee Fishing is known for its export-oriented black tiger shrimp farming; further details are available on the Lee Fishing page. Gems Group operates an integrated farming and processing business; see the Gems Group page for more information.

Challenges and Recommendations

Shrimp farming in Pahang faces risks from fluctuating water quality and disease. A white spot disease outbreak in 2022 caused substantial losses at some farms. Farmers are advised to adopt indoor recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS) to reduce the risk of disease transmission and establish water quality monitoring procedures. Automated feeding systems and solar-powered aeration equipment may also help improve farming efficiency. Investors considering entering the market are advised to select land near Kuantan or Temerloh, begin with a pilot operation of 5–10 hectares, and gradually expand to more than 30 hectares to achieve economies of scale.

Buying Direct from Farms vs. Wholesale Markets: A Buyer’s Guide

Choosing a sourcing channel is a critical decision for the success of a live seafood business. According to a 2023 report by the Malaysian Aquaculture Association, farm-direct prices are typically 15% to 30% lower than wholesale market prices, but involve higher logistics costs and greater time commitments.

Advantages and Limitations of Buying Direct from Farms

The main advantages of buying directly from farms are competitive pricing and traceability. Shrimp farms in Pahang offer wholesalers farm-gate prices of approximately RM25 to RM35 per kilogram, compared with final selling prices of RM32 to RM45 at wholesale markets. For buyers purchasing one metric tonne of fresh shrimp per month, direct sourcing can save approximately RM8,400 to RM12,000. However, farms typically require minimum orders of at least 500 kilograms, and delivery times depend on their operating schedules. This makes direct sourcing suitable for large restaurant groups, frozen food processors, and wholesalers.

Flexible Sourcing at Wholesale Markets

Wholesale markets offer the convenience of purchasing a wide variety of seafood on the spot. The Kuala Lumpur Seafood Wholesale Centre and Kuantan Central Market bring together suppliers from Sarawak, Sabah, and Pahang, allowing buyers to replenish multiple types of live seafood in a single day. However, prices increase as products pass through multiple intermediaries, while repeated handling reduces survival rates. Loss rates typically range from 5% to 15%.

Recommendations by Buyer Type

  • Small and medium-sized restaurants: Adopt a hybrid approach, using wholesale markets for small daily replenishments and sourcing directly from farms for larger weekly orders
  • Supermarkets and retailers: Build stable supply chains through partnerships with farms in Sarawak and operators such as Pasar Ren Joint Venture Group
  • Individual consumers: Visit shrimp farms in Kuantan or Temerloh on weekends to buy the freshest shrimp directly

The choice between buying directly from farms and sourcing from wholesale markets ultimately depends on purchasing volume, freshness requirements, and logistics capabilities.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I start sourcing directly from farms?

If you are sourcing directly from farms for the first time, start by contacting the Malaysian Aquaculture Association (MAI) for a list of accredited farms. Some farms in Sarawak and Sabah require buyers to visit in person, while farms in Pahang generally accept orders by phone or online. Begin with a small trial order, such as 50 kilograms, to assess product quality and logistics before gradually increasing your purchasing volume.

What is the minimum order quantity for direct farm purchases?

Requirements vary by farm. According to a 2023 market survey, shrimp farms in Pahang typically require a minimum order of 100 kilograms, while marine aquaculture farms in Sabah generally set a higher threshold of at least 500 kilograms for contracted wholesalers. Some farms charge an administrative fee or decline orders below the minimum quantity. Small restaurant operators can consider joint purchasing to reduce costs.

How can I maintain seafood quality during transport?

Live seafood is highly sensitive to transit time. A journey of approximately 3 to 4 hours from farms in Pahang to wholesale markets in Kuala Lumpur can maintain relatively high survival rates. Use oxygenated transport tanks and add ice for cooling during the summer. According to guidelines from the Fisheries Development Authority of Malaysia (FDAM), the ideal temperature for transporting live shrimp is 18 to 22°C. For journeys exceeding 6 hours, consider air freight instead of road transport.

Which farms hold quality certifications?

Approximately 35% of farms nationwide hold Good Aquaculture Practices (GMP) certification, with a higher proportion of around 45% in Pahang. Although prices at certified farms are typically 5% to 10% higher, certification helps ensure that drug residues comply with applicable standards, reducing food safety risks. Before purchasing, request inspection reports from the past three months.

How should small restaurants choose between direct farm sourcing and wholesale markets?

For restaurants purchasing less than 200 kilograms per month, buying from wholesale markets is generally more cost-effective. At low volumes, the logistics and time costs of direct farm sourcing can offset its price advantage. Wholesale markets offer flexible small orders, consolidated billing, and immediate collection. Use a monthly purchasing volume of 300 kilograms as a benchmark, and consider direct farm partnerships once your volume exceeds this level.

常見問題

What is the cost structure for importing live seafood from Malaysia to Macau?

The main costs include freight (approximately MOP 8–15 per kilogram), preservation and packaging (oxygen bags/ice: approximately MOP 3–5 per kilogram), import duties (some products qualify for duty exemptions), and inspection and quarantine fees (approximately MOP 500–800 per batch). For live grouper, total costs represent approximately 30–40% of the wholesale price.

What is the wholesale price of live Malaysian lobsters in the Macau market?

According to 2023 market data, the wholesale price of live Malaysian lobsters in Macau is approximately MOP 280–350 per 斤 (500 grams), depending on size and quality. Higher-quality lobsters weighing more than 500 grams can fetch over MOP 400, with a profit margin of approximately 25–35%.

What permits are required to import live seafood from Malaysia into Macau?

An import permit must be obtained from the Municipal Affairs Bureau. Importers must provide a health certificate from the country of origin, a quarantine inspection certificate, and proof of registration of the aquaculture farm in the exporting country. Additional permits are required for endangered species. The process should be initiated 2–3 weeks in advance.

How do the qualities of live grouper from Sarawak and Sabah in Malaysia differ?

Grouper farmed in deep-sea cages in Sarawak has firmer flesh and is suitable for sashimi. Grouper farmed in Sabah, where the seawater is clearer and cleaner, has a sweeter and more delicate flavor and is well suited to steaming. Sarawak offers a more reliable supply, while Sabah produces higher-quality fish but in smaller quantities.

How many hours does it take to transport live seafood from Malaysia to Macau?

Air freight takes approximately 3–4 hours from Kota Kinabalu or Sibu to Macau, followed by customs clearance and land transport, for a total delivery time of 6–8 hours. Sea freight takes 18–24 hours but has a lower survival rate. Air freight is recommended to preserve quality.

What is the demand for Malaysian spotted prawns in Macau's restaurant market?

Demand is steady, with approximately 70% of supply going to luxury hotels and seafood restaurants. Spotted prawns have consistent quality and are highly popular among Macau diners. Establishing long-term contracts with wholesalers is recommended to ensure a reliable supply and favorable pricing.

How should I select a supplier of live seafood from Malaysia?

Prioritize aquaculture farms certified by Malaysia's Department of Fisheries, and review their export history and quality inspection reports. Conducting site visits or working with reputable local traders is recommended to reduce intermediaries and lower costs.

Which types of Macau restaurants are most likely to purchase live seafood?

The primary target customers are mid- to high-end seafood restaurants, hotel buffets, wedding venues, and specialty seafood hotpot restaurants. These businesses place large orders for live seafood, place a strong emphasis on quality, and are willing to pay premium prices, making them an ideal customer segment.

How can AI help predict purchasing demand for live seafood?

An AI system can analyze historical sales data, seasonal and holiday patterns, weather changes, and market trends to forecast purchasing volumes over the next 7–14 days. This helps prevent excess or insufficient inventory. With an accuracy rate of over 85%, it can reduce spoilage costs.

What are the benefits of using an AI system to manage live seafood inventory?

An AI system can monitor survival rates and water-quality data in real time, identify the best time to sell inventory, and track the shelf life of each batch. It can also automatically generate purchasing recommendations to optimize inventory turnover, with an estimated 20–30% reduction in spoilage costs.
